# Three 15m strategies around the New York open (Nov 2026)

Generated by `research/nyopen/ny_report.js` from `ny_lab.js`. **Research only: the app, detector.js and the bot are unchanged.**

**The ask:** "make 3 new ones … best for 15 mins. I have seen mostly people trading before or in the first hour of the New York open by using SMC, try one of the strategy that way, backtest and check if any give consistent results."

**The three, all around the New York open** (the most active window for crypto: the US block averages 1.24× the range of the hours before it, ALGORITHM.md "Session overlay"), each from a different school:
1. **S1 Judas swing (SMC, ICT's New York morning model).** In the kill zone (08:30–11:00 ET) price sweeps the overnight low (00:00–08:30 ET), then a 15m close above the last swing high before that low (the market-structure shift) with a bullish fair value gap in the leg up. A limit buy at the gap's midpoint (until 12:00 ET), stop 0.1 × 15m ATR under the sweep low, target the overnight high (taken only when ≥ 2R away), out at 16:00 ET. Daily bias: the coin closed yesterday above its 20-day average.
2. **S2 Opening-range breakout on the coins in play (Zarattini & Aziz's equity day-trading method).** At 09:45 ET rank the coins by the volume of their 09:30–09:45 candle against that candle's last 14 weekdays. The top 5 (relative volume ≥ 1) whose opening candle closed green get a stop-buy at its high (until 15:00 ET), stop at its low, out at 16:00 ET, 20% of the pot each.
3. **S3 The 08:30 ET macro candle (CPI, payrolls, PPI…).** When BTC's 08:30–08:45 ET candle is ≥ 2 × its 15m ATR wide on ≥ 3 × that slot's usual volume, green and closing in its upper half: buy at its close (BTC; also ETH, and the 5 most liquid other coins), stop under its low, out at 11:00 ET.

**How it was tested.**
- Data: the user's 103 coins plus 171 delisted or collapsed ones (274; LUNA cut at 2022-05-14), full Binance 15m history, 2018-01-01 → 2026-11-05. A coin counts only with ≥ $5M a day (30-day average).
- The clock is New York's, with US daylight saving (EST/EDT), weekdays only. Long only (spot).
- Everything was fixed from the trading logic before any result was seen; each variant changes ONE thing.
- Fills:
  - A limit buy fills at its price only when price trades below it.
  - A stop-buy fills at its level or a worse open.
  - On the fill candle, a stop counts first and a target cannot fill.
  - Stops fill at the stop or a worse open.
- Costs (column "a trade"): 0.1% fee a side. Market orders, stop-buys, stops and timed exits also pay 0.05% slippage on coins trading ≥ $100M a day, 0.15% on the rest. "Before costs" removes all costs; "futures costs" is 0.05% a side plus 0.4× the slippage.
- Controls: the same rules at other times of day, random coins, and other "opens".
- "Pot": 20% of the pot a trade, at most 5 a day (S3 BTC/ETH: 100%, one trade). Every trade is flat by the day's end, so drawdown is read on daily marks.

## Verdict: none of the three is worth adding

1. **The SMC Judas swing loses, before costs too.** 772 trades, 29% winners, −0.58% a trade after costs and −0.26% before. Every variant loses after costs: kill-zone timing, London or Asia lows, entry at the gap top or the shift close, 2R/3R targets, BTC bias, an earlier exit. The same rules 6, 12 and 18 hours later do as well or better (12 hours later: −0.03% a trade). The New York timing adds nothing, and the setup itself has no edge. That is the seventh sweep-and-reverse setup this repo has rejected (ALGORITHM.md "REJECTED — do not rebuild").
2. **The opening-range breakout loses harder than buying random coins.** 4139 trades, 26% winners, −0.65% a trade (−0.18% before costs). The same breakout on 5 random liquid coins: −0.52% (−0.05% before costs). The 00:00 UTC and London opens are no better. Unusual opening volume picks volatile coins, not their direction, the same lesson as the Oct 2026 volume-burst study.
3. **The macro candle is the only one with a hint of an edge, and it is too small and rare to trade.**
   - Before costs, ETH and the alts beat the same big candle at random times of day: ETH +0.72% vs +0.17%, p 0.010; the 5 liquid alts +0.59% vs +0.16%, p 0.033. BTC's lead is not significant (+0.35% vs +0.12%, p 0.17).
   - It shows again on the 10:00 ET candle, which played no part in designing it: ETH p 0.048, alts p 0.043, on 19 events. It does not appear on the 14:00 ET (FOMC) candle. With this many looks, that is suggestive, not proven.
   - Even if it is real, it is small. After spot costs, ETH makes +0.39% a trade on 41 events in 8.8 years, about +1.8% a year with all of the pot on each trade. BTC makes +0.05%, the alts +0.24%.
4. **The New York open is not directional in crypto.** BTC and ETH move less than ±6 bp on average in each half-hour from 08:30 to 11:00 ET. None of those half-hours is significant, and their signs flip between periods (table below). A spot round trip costs 20–50 bp. The open is busy, not biased, so a routine that trades it every day pays its costs and gets nothing back.

## What this means

- **A routine that trades every New York morning gets nothing back for its costs.** The open adds volatility, not direction. S1 and S2 lose before costs, and the costs then add 0.3–0.5% a trade.
- **The 15m edges this repo has found are events.** The BTC flush playbook (research/flush/) and the macro candle are both real, and both are rare: about 10 and about 5 a year. The other 15m result that works, `H4e`, is the 4h breakout read on 15m candles (research/s15/).
- If the macro candle is ever used, it fits as a heads-up next to those, not as a strategy of its own. It needs a reaction within minutes of 08:45 ET, which GitHub's scheduler cannot give the bot.
