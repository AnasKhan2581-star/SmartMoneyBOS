## The short answer

Each strategy is **context** (read once a day, the same on every chart) plus a **trigger** (read on the chart). "Book" is 10 equal slots, so at most 10 stocks are held at once. "Beats random days" asks whether the trigger did better than buying on random days when the same context was met and holding just as long: "clearly" is 3 or more standard errors on all history and ahead in every sub-period, "a little" 1.5 or more and ahead in every sub-period.

## What was tested

- **US: 50 stocks** (40 already listed in 2000, 50 with hourly candles). Fifty large companies picked by hand from names that Shariah-screened US funds commonly hold. **Nobody verified their compliance here; screens change every quarter, so check each one before trading it.** Sectors: Technology 16, Communication 2, Consumer discretionary 6, Consumer staples 4, Healthcare 10, Industrials 4, Energy 4, Materials 4.
- **India: 97 stocks** (59 already listed in 2010, 96 with hourly candles). Every stock on the HalalVest page. Sectors: Chemicals 14, Capital goods 23, Healthcare 13, Electronics 7, Consumer 8, Auto 8, Logistics 4, Energy & metals 9, IT 11.
- **Prices:** Yahoo Finance, adjusted for splits and dividends. Daily candles: US from 2000, India from 2010. Yahoo's Indian history before 2008 is full of mis-adjusted splits, so anything before a one-day move of more than 45% is discarded (the notes at the end list each case).
- **Hourly candles reach back only to December 2023** (Yahoo serves 730 days). After a warm-up the three charts are compared from 2024-02-01 on. 15-minute candles reach back 60 days: nothing can be tested on them. Hourly volume is unreliable (India reports none for the first hour of most days), so no hourly rule uses it.
- **Costs:** US 0.05% a side (spread and slippage; commissions are zero). India 0.2% a side (0.1% STT on delivery trades, exchange and stamp charges, slippage). Taxes are not counted.
- **Fills:** a signal is read at a candle's close and filled at the next candle's open. A stop fills at its level, or at the open when the candle opens beyond it.
- **The book:** 10 equal slots. When more signals arrive than slots are free, the stock with the stronger 6-month return goes first.
- **Every test window starts fresh:** nothing bought before its first day counts, and a rule already in a buy state that day buys at the first open. Trend therefore starts fully invested, so a window that opens near a top (January 2000 in the US) shows it.
- **Random days (the control):** for every trade, 20 random candles of the same stock in the same years where the same context was met, each held as long as the real trade. The trigger's edge is the real average minus that average.

## The four strategies

**Context**, read once a day at the close. An hourly candle reads yesterday's close, so nothing looks ahead.
- **M, market:** the S&P 500 (or the Sensex) is above its 200-day average.
- **T, trend:** the stock is above its own 200-day average.
- **R, strength:** the stock's 6-month return is in the top half of its list.
- **S, sector:** the stock's sector is in the top half of sectors by 3-month return. A sector is the list's own stocks in it.

**Triggers**, counted in candles of the chart. The same rule is therefore a different horizon on each chart: 50 candles are about 7 days on hourly, 10 weeks on daily and a year on weekly.

| | Context | Buy | Sell |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Leaders** | M | At each month's end rank the list by its 12-month return, skipping the latest month. Hold the top fifth in equal parts, each only if above its own 200-day average. | A stock leaves when it drops out of the top 30%. Everything is sold when the market test fails at a month's end. |
| **Trend** | M, R | The 50-candle average is above the 200-candle average and the close is above the 200. | The 50 falls under the 200, or a stop 25% under the entry. |
| **Breakout** | M, T, R | The first close above the high of the last 50 candles, on 1.5× the usual volume (daily and weekly). | A close under the low of the last 50 candles, or a stop 3 × ATR under the entry. |
| **Pullback** | M, T | The 2-candle RSI is under 10: a sharp dip in a stock that is still in an uptrend. | The first close above the 5-candle average, or after 10 candles, or a stop 5 × ATR under the entry. |

ATR is the stock's average candle range over 14 candles. RSI under 10 on 2 candles means two hard down candles in a row.

- **Breakout:** the first draft sold on a 20-candle low. Selling on a 50-candle low held winners longer (+13.4% a year against +9.1%). No variant beat random days by much: the trigger is a way to get into strong stocks, not an edge of its own.
- **Pullback:** every variant beat random days, so the plain one was kept. A stop 5 × ATR away cost nothing; a stop at 3 × ATR did.
- **The gap idea** (a gap up of 1.5 × ATR that holds, on twice the usual volume) fires about 0.24 times a stock a year in large companies and beat random days by only +0.31%. Dropped.
- **Leaders:** the textbook 12-months-skip-one ranking was kept even though 6 of the 10 other variants scored higher here per unit of worst fall (6-1 months; 3 months, no skip; top tenth; top third; no own-trend test; no keep band). Picking the best cell of a table is how backtests fool people.

## Limits

- **Hindsight in the lists** (see the top). It lifts every row, and it lifts "buy the dip" most: a dip in a company known to have survived always came back.
- **The US list's Shariah compliance is not verified.** The Indian list is the HalalVest page's own screen.
- **Hourly results cover under three years**, and 15-minute charts could not be tested at all.
- **The book is path-dependent.** Which trades find a free slot changes its growth by several points a year, so differences of that size between two rows mean little. The per-trade columns are steadier.
- **Taxes are not counted.** They fall hardest on the short trades (Pullback).
- **One data source.** Yahoo's adjusted prices; Indian history before 2010 is not used.
- **Leaders sold everything on one month-end reading of the market.** The exact day matters: weekly and quarterly readings did much worse in the US design years.
- **Order of work.** The Trend stop and the deeper dip rule were added after the first full run had been seen. Both were still chosen from the design years' numbers alone, but a rule added late deserves less trust than one fixed at the start.
- **A late fix to the test itself.** The first runs let a window inherit positions bought before it and then left them out, so Trend never started fully invested and its fall after the 2000 bubble did not show. Every window now starts fresh. The rules were not re-chosen after that fix.
