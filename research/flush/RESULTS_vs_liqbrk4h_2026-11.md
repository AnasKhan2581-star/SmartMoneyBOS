## What it says

1. **liqbrk makes more money; the flush loses far less.** On your alert list liqbrk grows the pot ×15.1 (+34.6% a year) against the flush's ×5.36 (+20.1%). It falls −70% on the way against −12%. Per unit of drawdown the flush earns about three times as much (1.63 vs 0.49). liqbrk wins 36% of trades and the flush 75%.
2. **liqbrk depends on the coin list; the flush doesn't.**
   - On your alert list liqbrk makes ×15.1, and on your 103 Compare coins ×39.0. Both lists were picked in 2026 from coins that are still alive, and its big years come from a few runners (2023: CFX +166%, SUPER +114%, ARK +109% a trade, each in a 20% slot).
   - On every coin that was tradeable at the time, including the 171 that later died, the same book ends at ×0.02: −87% in 2022, −60% in 2023, −90% in 2024.
   - Why: 47% of its signals came from coins that later died, and the book takes the loudest breakout first. A dying coin's pump is loud.
   - The flush barely notices the dead coins (×5.36 with them, ×4.88 without): it buys a capitulation dip for hours, not a breakout for days.
   - So liqbrk's real-world result rests on choosing coins that survive, and the ×15.1 carries hindsight.
3. **They hardly overlap.** Monthly correlation 0.09 with the alert-list book (0.18 on all coins); the flush's correlation with BTC is 0.00, liqbrk's 0.42. In liqbrk's five worst months (alert list) the flush had no trade in 4 and made −4.1% in the other.
4. **Run together in one pot, the flush adds its return on money that would sit idle.** liqbrk is in the market 41% of the time; when a flush comes it got 87% of its full size on average. The alert-list pot goes from ×15.1 to ×57.6 (+55.8% a year) at a max drawdown of −69% instead of −70%. Splitting the money 50/50 instead cuts the drawdown to −41% but earns less (×12.0), because half of it waits for flushes.
