## Verdict

1. **Yes, there is one that makes money on 15m, and it is the 4h breakout read on 15m candles.** `H4e` keeps every rule of the 4h Liquidity Breakout and buys at the FIRST 15m close at which the 4h candle forming so far already meets them all. Exits stay the 4h ones. Over all history: median coin +23.8% (+18.0% at 0.10% slippage a side), 65/103 coins profitable, win rate 38%, PF 1.52, positive in all four two-year blocks (+2.62% · +2.08% · +2.05% · +1.40% a trade) and in both halves of the coin list (+22.1% · +41.8%). It makes about what the 4h chart makes (+24.7%, +20.8% at 0.10%) with 43% more trades and entries up to 3¾ hours earlier, at a deeper median drawdown (−47% against −39%).
2. **The shipped 15m Breakout is weak because three of its rules count CANDLES, not days.** On 15m they become different, jumpier rules than the 4h ones that carry the edge. The volume test reads ONE 15m candle against its daily average, so a single busy candle passes. The stop is 4× the range of 15m candles, so it sits inside ordinary intraday noise. And the trail sells on any 15m close under the prior day's low, even one that recovers within the hour. Shipped: median coin +3.6%, win rate 28%, PF 1.09. Judging volume over the last 4 hours alone takes it to +10.1%. With the stop from 4h ranges, the trail judged at 4h closes and BTC also above its 149-bar MA (`Vsxb`): +21.5%, win rate 37%, PF 1.47, 23 coins ✓. But it trades twice as often as 4h, so at 0.10% slippage its median coin falls to +3.4%. It is also weakest in 2025–26 (+0.55% a trade).
3. **What the 15m chart cannot do is add edge of its own.** Buying early (H4e) gives the same money as waiting for the 4h close, plus more risk. A limit buy on a 15m retest of the broken level gives better-priced trades but misses the runners: bought at market when no retest comes, the median coin makes +18.2% against +24.7%. Every nudge of the 15m-native version that reads the market more slowly (8-hour volume, a trail at 8h closes) does better. The edge lives on the 4h-to-daily scale.
4. **Tested and rejected on 15m:**
   - Order flow, a new source: the share of volume that buyers took at the ask, on the entry candle, over 4h and over 1 day, plus trade count and trade size. No measure rises steadily from the most seller-heavy fifth of trades to the most buyer-heavy. The one hint fails a shuffle test (section 4).
   - Buying a sweep of the prior day's low in an uptrend: 26,428 trades, PF 0.95, median coin −25.9%.
   - From earlier studies: day trades, volume-burst scalps, top-gainer rotation, dip and fade strategies, session plays.
   - The reason is the same each time: a round trip costs ~0.2–0.3%, and moves that last minutes to hours are mostly noise of about that size.
5. **Recommendation:** for 15m charts, alerts and Compare rows, run Liquidity Breakout with its 4h rules (`H4e`, or `H4c` to wait for the 4h close: the same trades as the 4h chart). Not shipped; it needs your go-ahead.

## 4. Order flow: does aggressive buying mark the real breakouts?

For every trade, the buyer share at entry is put in quintiles (Q1 = the most seller-heavy fifth, Q5 = the most buyer-heavy). A real effect would rise from Q1 to Q5, in every block.

Shuffling bottom-fifth/rest labels 5,000 times inside each block: L0 +0.23% vs +0.22% (chance 52%) · Vsxb +2.07% vs +1.51% (chance 92%) · H4e +1.29% vs +2.11% (chance 11%) · H4 +1.91% vs +2.70% (chance 19%). That is chance, even before allowing for the ~20 tries (and the 15m strategies point the other way). **Order flow at the entry carries no usable edge here.**
