With the shipped exit it reproduces `research/liqbrk_lab.js`'s shipped rules trade-for-trade on all 61 alert coins (`node research/smcexit/smcexit_lab.js parity`). Entries are the shipped liqbrk entries in every row (149-bar MA, 1×ATR decisive break, BTC-50d gate / +50% leader rescue); only the exit changes. Fills are honest: next candle's open ± 0.05% slippage, 0.1% fee per side, a target fills at max(open, level), and when the stop and a target fall in the same candle the stop is taken first. Money: all-in per trade, every candle marked to market.

**The question (user, Oct 2026):** instead of the fixed "sell half at +3R, then let the ATR stop / trail close the rest", take profit where SMC says price is likely to fall back — at resting **buy-side liquidity** above (swing highs not yet traded through) or at a **major bearish FVG**, the supply POI shorts sell from.

**How the levels are drawn — never from the future:**
- **Swing highs** come from the engine's own zigzag (a 4×ATR reversal, `extMult`) and are known only from the candle that confirmed the reversal.
- **BSL** = a swing high price has not traded above yet. It is live from that confirmation until the first later wick above it (the sweep).
- **Major bearish FVG** = a 3-candle gap down (low two candles back > this candle's high) at least 0.5×ATR tall (`fvgMult`). It is known at the close
  of the third candle and live until price first trades back up into it; the target is its bottom edge, where it is first reached.
- A trade's target is the nearest live level above the entry **as known at the signal candle's close**, fixed for the life of the trade.
  "POI" = the nearer of the two kinds. "≥ 2R up" skips levels closer than 2R for the next one up. No level above → the shipped +3R.

## 2. Taking profit at the level instead of at +3R

**The test a change has to pass, on BOTH universes** (the cycle rebuild's bar, plus a control): it leaves more coins richer than poorer, it cuts drawdown on more coins than it deepens it, its profit factor is at least the shipped one's both over the trades in the first half of each coin's history and over those in the second half, and it beats its **control** — the same rule with the SMC information removed. For a target, the control draws each trade's distance at random from the same coin's own SMC target distances, so it sells just as close or far, at a level with no structure behind it. For the FVG-reaction exit, the control uses fake FVGs: every real one moved 1–2 ATR up or down. The **test** column reads net · DD · halves · control.

Σ ln(1+net) is the whole universe compounded (one unit in every coin), so it is the fairest single total; "median net" is the typical coin. "level found" = trades that had a level above them at the signal close; "reached" = of those, how many got there.

## 4. Reading it

- **Resting BSL is where the move continues, not where it turns.** After a sweep, price fell 1 ATR before it rose 1 ATR in 53% of cases, against 62% at plain levels (oos: 58% vs 65%). The next day was weaker than at plain levels on only 21 of 53 alert coins and 61 of 207 oos coins. Inside a breakout trade, the liquidity above is fuel, not a ceiling.
- **A breakout entry almost always has an old swing high just above it** (96% of trades, median 0.6R away), so "½ at BSL" really means "½ almost at once". That lifts the win rate (37% → 45%; oos 31% → 39%) and cuts the median drawdown (−53% → −45%; oos −45% → −39%), but lowers the profit factor (1.39 → 1.35; oos 0.89 → 0.83) — and random levels at the same distances do better than the BSL on both universes (Σ 8.4 vs 5.6; oos −68.9 vs −74.4). What helps the drawdown is selling half early, not the BSL.
- **Bearish FVGs are no better than any level as a turning point** (−1 ATR first 64% vs 62%; oos 62% vs 65%; next day weaker than at plain levels on 15 of 31 and 28 of 62 coins — a coin flip), yet the three FVG exits beat their controls in 6 of 6 comparisons: the only SMC input here with any information in it. Against the shipped exit the gain is small and not stable. On the alert coins the PF is 1.42 / 1.38 / 1.40 vs 1.39, but in the first half of each coin's history it is 1.278 / 1.237 / 1.240 vs 1.281: they help in the later half of each coin's history and hurt in the earlier half.
- **On the oos coins liqbrk loses money** (shipped PF 0.89), so any rule that gets out sooner loses less there. That is why most rows beat the shipped exit on oos Σ, and why the controls decide. Example: selling ALL at the nearest BSL improves oos Σ (−82.4 → −62.9), random levels do it better (−55.2), and on the alert coins, where liqbrk makes money, the same rule wrecks it (Σ 6.7 → −0.1, PF 1.39 → 1.16): it sells the trades that would have run.
