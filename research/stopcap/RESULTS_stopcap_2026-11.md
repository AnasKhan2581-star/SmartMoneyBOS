# The Breakout rule after INIT's −41.7% stop-out: pump entries and a stop with no limit (Nov 2026)

Generated by `research/stopcap/stopcap_lab.js report`. The study itself changed nothing. **Both proposed rules were then approved by the user and shipped (Nov 2026):** no leader exception, and no buy when the stop would be more than 25% under the entry. In the tables below, "shipped" means the rules the study measured: the Oct 2026 rules, with the leader exception and no stop limit.

**The report (user):** "The last trade that happened actually got stopped out at -41%, no trader would trade like that, there are things still wrong in the strategy."

**Short answer: two rule gaps, both measurable.**
- **The entry.** While BTC is weak, the only buys allowed are "leaders" (+50% in 30 days). A two-day pump makes a coin a leader, so the rule ends up buying the top of pumps.
- **The stop.** It is 4 × ATR, with no upper limit. After a pump that is 30–45% of the price.
- Skipping those trades improves the average trade on all three coin sets (+1.72% → +2.16% / +2.22% → +2.29% / +0.62% → +0.95%), and no trade in the history then loses more than about 25%.
- A tighter stop on the same trades does not help. A smaller buy caps the loss too, but earns less per trade than skipping on all three sets (+1.83% against +2.16% / +2.13% against +2.29% / +0.86% against +0.95%).

## 4. Keeping the trade: a tighter stop, or a smaller buy

Two things a trader might do instead of skipping.
- **A tighter stop.** Every trade is taken, but its stop is never more than 20% (or 25%) under the entry.
- **A smaller buy.** The stop stays where it is, and the buy shrinks so that a stop-out costs at most 10% (15%, 20%) of a slot. With a 30% stop and a 15% limit, half a slot is bought.

Results are in slot units (size × return), so a half-size trade that loses 30% counts as −15%. "End differently" is: trades whose result changes · the average change. "Slot used" is the average size bought.

## Proposal (items 1 and 2 shipped in Nov 2026, as written)

1. **In a weak market, no Breakout buys.** Drop the leader exception: a breakout is bought only while BTC is over its 50-day average.
   - Milder alternative: keep the exception only for coins that were already leaders a day earlier. Over history it does about the same; of the four recent losers it would have skipped two, not all four.
2. **No buy when the stop would be more than 25% under the entry.** The stop stays 4 × ATR; when that is more than 25% of the price, the signal is skipped.
   - This is a risk rule more than a profit rule. Over 30% the trades lose. Between 25% and 30% the coin sets disagree.
   - Alternative: 30%. It costs nothing on any set, and leaves a worst trade near −30%.
3. **Not proposed now: the smaller buy** (section 4). It is the stricter risk rule (no stop-out costs more than 15% of a slot) for a little less money, and it needs sizes in the alerts and the book. It can be added on top later.

**Limits.**
- Both ideas come from looking at losing trades, INIT's first. The cut-offs were tried next to their neighbours (30%, 20%, 15%), and both halves of history were in view, so none of this is an untouched test.
- Wide-stop trades are few and fat-tailed. Skipping them gives up the occasional pump that keeps running: on the 103-coin list the 25–30% band made money.
- The rule still loses on about 6 trades in 10 and the pot still draws down more than 50%. This removes the worst kind of trade, not the drawdowns.
- A stop can be jumped: a candle that opens under the stop fills at the open. No trade in this history did that after the 25% limit, but it can happen.
- One strategy, 4h candles. Trend Follow, Turtle and the Cycle have their own exits and are not touched.
