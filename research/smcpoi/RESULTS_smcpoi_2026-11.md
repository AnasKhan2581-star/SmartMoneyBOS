# The user's 15m SMC continuation pattern (Nov 2026)

Generated by `research/smcpoi/poi_lab.js report`. **Research only: detector.js, the webapp and the bot are unchanged.**

**The ask** (with a chart):
- major higher highs, then a sudden drop and a sudden up move that closes above the last major high;
- the coin doesn't run much, and comes back to a POI inside that up move;
- trade it with a small stop and high RR, with higher-timeframe liquidity deciding whether to take it;
- not too few trades;
- think through the logic, backtest, modify, rethink if needed (e.g. only around the New York open).

**The logic.**
- In a 15m uptrend the stops sit under the last higher low.
- A sudden drop through it fills big buyers against forced sellers; a sudden close above the last major high shows the drop was a trap.
- The up move leaves unfilled orders (fair value gaps) that price returns to.
- Buying there with the stop under the sweep low is cheap risk, and the high of the up move (the buy stops above it) is the target.
- The known danger is adverse selection: strong moves don't come back, so a resting limit fills mostly on the weak ones.

**How it was tested.**
- Data: 273 coins (the user's 103 plus 171 delisted or collapsed), full Binance 15m history, 2018-01-01 → 2026-11-05. A coin counts only on days it trades ≥ $5M (30-day average).
- Fills: a limit buy fills only when price trades below it; on the fill candle the stop counts first and the target cannot fill; stops fill at the stop or a worse open.
- Costs: 0.1% fee a side, plus 0.05% slippage on stops and timed exits (coins above $100M a day) or 0.15% (the rest).
- Halves: rules were meant to be chosen on 2018–21 and judged on 2022–26. Every round printed both halves side by side, so 2022–26 is **not a pristine test**; round 8 was designed after seeing round 7.
- **Control:** every trade's exact order (entry depth below the price, stop and target in R) is placed at 20 random times on the same coin.
- Random numbers come from `research/rng.js` (mulberry32).
  - The first version of this report drew the controls and bootstraps from a generator that loops after a few thousand draws: `seed * 1103515245` overflows JavaScript's 53-bit numbers.
  - The trades themselves never used it. The controls and p-values here are the corrected ones.

## The rules (the final version)

1. **Structure.** An ATR zigzag on 15m candles: a swing counts once price reverses 3 × the 15m ATR. The last two swing highs and lows must be rising (higher highs, higher lows). H0 = the last major high, L0 = the higher low before it.
2. **The drop.** Within 8 hours of H0, price trades below L0 (the sell stops under the higher low).
3. **The up move.** Within 6 hours of the drop's low, a 15m candle CLOSES above H0.
4. **HTF liquidity (the deciding context).** Either the drop took the previous day's low, or the previous day's high is still untaken above the up move. Otherwise no trade.
5. **The POI.** Going down from the top of the up move, the first bullish fair value gap whose middle still pays ≥ 2R to the move's high. The limit buy rests at that middle for 12 hours.
6. **The stop.** 0.1 × the 15m ATR under the sweep low, and only if it is 1–5% below the entry.
   - Under 1%, the ~0.3% of fees and slippage are a third of the stop or more. Those trades lost in both halves in every version.
   - Over 5% is not a small stop.
7. **The exit.** Target: the high of the up move (as of the fill). Out after 24 hours if neither is hit. One trade per coin at a time.

## Verdict

**The pattern is real.**
- The same orders at random times lose 0.39% a trade, and the bare pattern does 0.35% better.
- With the HTF context, the first paying gap and a 1–5% stop, it beats its random-times control by 0.63% a trade, in both halves.

**What made it pay, in the order found:**
1. **The HTF liquidity context.** The drop took yesterday's low, or yesterday's high waits above.
2. **A V-shaped up move bought at its first gap.** The quality sits in moves whose gaps are all low: a sharp V off the sweep low, then a clean run through H0.
3. **A stop of at least 1%.** On 15m candles the fees and slippage are fixed in %, so a small stop is mostly cost. The user's "small stop" works here only as "1–5%".

**What failed:**
- The stop under the gap: stopped out ~80% of the time.
- Deep POIs (the order block, 79%): they fill on the moves that fail.
- Fixed 2R/3R/5R targets.
- Only the New York morning: it cuts trades without adding edge.
- 4h pools as the context: they almost never filter.
- A confirmation close above the gap: far fewer trades, weaker on 2022–26.
- Selling half at +1R: the win rate rises to 56–63%, but the money falls.

**Why it is not worth adding.**
- **It makes its money on coins that were later delisted.** On the user's 103 coins the final version made 237 trades at −0.03% a trade: the pattern still beats random there (0.27% lost a trade at random times), but only back to zero after costs. Every version earns far less on the user's coins. The best there, the selective version, made +0.44% a trade on only 35 trades (a 25% chance the true average is ≤ 0).
- **It is small.** Across all 273 coins, at 20% of the pot a trade, it adds +3.6% a year (max drawdown −8%). The selective version earns more a trade (+1.06%) but trades 10 times a year, 35 times on the user's coins in 8.8 years.
- **Many rules were tried** (about 80 variants in 8 rounds), and 2022–26 was in view the whole time.
- **The bot can't run it as is.** It needs a limit order placed within minutes of a 15m close, and GitHub runs the bot hours late.
