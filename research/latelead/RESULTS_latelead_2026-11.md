# Sudden runs and the Breakout rule: INIT's +79% day (Nov 2026)

Generated by `research/latelead/latelead_lab.js report`. **Research only: detector.js, the webapp and the bot are unchanged.**

**The report (user, with INIT/USDT 4h at +79% in 24 hours):** "No strategy out of all still are able to detect and alerts and trade for these kind of sudden runs."

**Short answer.**
- The Breakout rule did detect INIT's run. The BTC filter kept it out, on purpose.
- Every way of buying such runs was tested on all history, and each adds losing trades.
- The rule already buys some sudden runs, late, through its "leader" exception. Those trades lost too, on average. The data argues for buying fewer of them, not more.

1. **10 Nov 01:30 IST, close 0.1694.** The +30% candle passed every chart rule: over the 2-day high by more than 1 ATR, over the 5-day and 149-candle averages, volume over 1.3× the day's.
   - The market filter blocked it. BTC was under its 50-day average, and INIT was +44% over 30 days, short of the +50% that makes a coin a "leader".
2. **One candle later** INIT was a leader, so the filter opened. But that close was no longer the FIRST close above the 2-day high, and the rule only buys a fresh breakout.
3. **10 Nov 13:30 IST, close 0.2228.** The filter was open (leader) and the close was over the 2-day high (0.2140), but not by a full ATR: it needed 0.2323. No buy. The candle after it cannot buy either, because it would not be a fresh breakout.
4. **From here** the rule needs a close back at or under the 2-day high, then a new breakout over it by 1 ATR. While INIT stays a "leader" it would buy that one: a late buy, far above where the run began.

The other strategies are slow by design and not built for this: Turtle buys a 55-day high, Trend Follow needs the 200-day average, the Cycle reads weekly averages.

- A pump in a weak market has a 4×ATR stop that is 20–25% away, so each failure costs a lot.

- **"A leader a day earlier too" drops 37 / 39 / 128 trades that averaged −2.67% / −2.15% / −4.19%.** The average trade, both profit factors and the pot improve in all three sets, and four to five times as many coins gain as lose.
- It has no number to tune: it reads the same +50%/30-day test one day back.
- **The pot figures are noisy.** Buying every shipped trade one candle late moves the alert-list pot from ×12.6 to ×28.7, only because different trades find a free slot. The per-trade columns are the evidence.
- Dropping the exception altogether does about as well. Leaders that were already leaders are roughly break-even: positive on the two lists, negative with the delisted coins.

## Verdict

- **The system saw INIT and stayed out because BTC was weak.** That filter is right on average: the breakouts it blocks lose as a group, and the pump-day ones lose most.
- **No rule that buys these runs earlier holds up** across the three coin sets.
- **One change is worth proposing, in the other direction:** the leader exception should need the coin to have been a leader a day earlier too. It stops the late chase after a one-day pump, which is what the rule would have done on INIT next.
- **Limits.** The dropped trades are 37–128 in nine years, so the gain per year is small, and on the alert coins alone the odds that they are no worse than ordinary trades are about 1 in 5. Both ideas were written after looking at INIT. The Oct 2026 study that shipped the leader exception used 32 coins; this one uses 65–272.
