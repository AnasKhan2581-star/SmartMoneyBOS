# ScreenerPro (SmartMoneyBOS)

Long-only daily **crypto investment advisor webapp**. Fully static — no build step, no deps.

## Files
- Chart tab opens on **ARBUSDT · 4h · 4500 bars · liqbrk** (user's default combo; TF moved 2h→4h Oct 2026, when the 2h alerts were dropped) — the same coin and TF the signal bot watches.
- `index.html` — UI: four tabs (**Portfolio** = the default home screen / **Alerts** / Chart / **Compare** — all strategies × a user-editable symbol list), strategy dropdown, Advisor panel, equity backtest, mobile-first: the Portfolio screen's phone styles are the BASE, a `@media(min-width:700px)` block upgrades it to multi-column, and a fixed bottom tab bar replaces the header nav under 700px. All media queries stay at the END of `<style>` so they win the cascade. Fetches Binance spot klines client-side (`api.binance.com`, fallback `data-api.binance.vision`).
- **Fetch by bars OR by date range** (Oct 2026, both tabs): `#rangeMode` / `#cmpRangeMode` switch between a bar count and `From`/`To` date inputs. `fetchKlines(sym, tf, spec, onPage)` takes `spec` as either a bar COUNT (number) or a RANGE (`{from,to}` in ms) — in range mode it pages back until it passes `from` **or the pair's history runs out**, so a start date older than the listing silently yields the earliest bars that exist. That clamp is always reported, never hidden: the Chart status reads `… · SOLUSDT only lists from 2020-08-11`, and Compare collects them into `short[]` → `listed later, clamped to earliest: SOL from 2020-08-11, AVAX from 2020-09-22`. **Rows clamped this way cover less history than their neighbours — the Yrs column is what makes two rows comparable.** In date mode Compare still needs the 10k reference, so it fetches back to `min(from, to − CMP_REF_BARS × TF_MS[tf])` and slices both windows out of the one result. `cmpRowKey()` is the single source of the row-cache key for both modes — never rebuild it inline, that is how `renderCmp` and `runCompare` once read different keys.
- `runCompare` **queues** a request that arrives while one is in flight (`cmpPending`) instead of dropping it. Dropping it left the table rendering a key nobody had computed — reachable by switching to date mode (which fires a fetch) and then picking dates a moment later.
- **Compare tab** (Sep 2026): timeframes **15m/1h/2h/4h/1d/1w** (15m added Oct 2026). It **opens on All strategies · 4h · 19,096 bars (≈8.7 years) with Window sweep on**, the user's own settings (Oct 2026): `#cmpStrat` defaults to `all`, `CMP_BARS['4h']` = 19096, and 15m starts at 35,040 bars (≈1 year, 36 pages a coin, the heaviest pull this tab makes). Symbol list is editable (`#cmpSyms`, comma-separated, bare tickers get `USDT` appended, persisted in `localStorage` under `smb.cmpSyms`, `Reset list` restores `CMP_DEFAULT`). **`CMP_DEFAULT` is the user's own 103-coin list (Oct 2026), in their order.** XMR (data ends 2024-02-20) and LIT (Litentry, ends 2025-02-10) are delisted, so their rows grade history only up to the delisting. A fresh browser opening Compare therefore starts a 103-coin run that takes minutes, not seconds; browser tests set a short `smb.cmpSyms` first; unreachable/delisted symbols are named in `#cmpStatus` rather than silently dropped. **Bar count is user-set** (`#cmpBars`, 300–60000, persisted under `smb.cmpBars`, live "≈ N days/mo/yrs" readout in `#cmpSpan` that turns red under 200 days): it tracks `CMP_BARS[tf]` until the user types a number, then stays pinned across TF changes (`cmpBarsTouched`). `cmpCache` is keyed `sym|tf` storing `{rows, asked}` so lowering the count slices what is already held instead of refetching, and a symbol with genuinely short history is not refetched forever; `cmpRowsTf` is keyed `tf|bars`. The 300 floor exists because `runCompare` skips anything under 250 bars — don't lower it or the box can select a guaranteed-empty table. `#cmpStrat` options are **generated from `CMP_STRATS`** — never hand-write them again, that is how `composite`/`meanrev` survived their July 2026 removal as dead options. **Window sweep** (`#cmpSweep`, on by default) slides a window of 35% of history across the equity path inside `pathStats` — no extra engine runs, no extra fetches, so toggling it is a pure re-render. **Every run measures TWO windows**: the user's bar count *and* a fixed `CMP_REF_BARS`=10,000 reference, both sliced from one fetch (`need = max(bars, 10000)`) so the reference costs no extra requests — which also means raising the Bars box anywhere below 10,000 never refetches. Rows carry `{e:recent, d:reference, sameWin}`. **`#cmpPick`** renders the go-to combo above the table: it requires the FULL investment test (`okRow`, shared with the table's ✓) on the **reference**, plus only a form check on the recent window (`n>=3 && net>0 && edge>0`) — a short window cannot carry the trade floor when `liqbrk` takes ~7 trades/year on 4h, so demanding it there would fail every window on arithmetic rather than merit. Ranked by **reference** edge, never the noisy recent one. The panel is a hero figure + KPI rows (not a chart — it is a handful of headline numbers for one pick), states the timeframe, mutes any stat from a window below `minTrades()` with a `*`, and must keep its two caveats: the windows **overlap** (recent sits inside the reference, so it is not two independent tests) and everything is a backtest. When nothing qualifies it says so and names the closest miss — never invent a pick.
Columns (Oct 2026, rebuilt for investment comparison — CAGR/6M/1Y/Sharpe removed): Vol · Trades · **Net P/L** · **vs B&H** · PF · Max DD · Win rate · **In mkt** · Stable · Yrs · ✓. **✓ is sortable** (`data-k="ok"`): ticked rows first, each group ordered by vs B&H; a second click reverses. `vs B&H` = `(1+net)/(1+bh)−1`, how much richer you ended than holding the same coin over the same window. ✓ requires `net>0 AND edge>0 AND PF≥1.3 AND WR≥30% AND DD≤60% AND closed trades ≥ `minTrades()` — **12 on 1h and below, 20 above** (user decision Oct 2026, was 30; see ALGORITHM.md for the noise this accepts) AND ≥70% of windows profitable`. On 1h only `liqbrk` produces a real sample (Cycle needs a 200-week MA; Trend/Turtle need months per trade), which is why those rows show 0–2 trades.
- **Top 100 button** (`#cmpTop100`, Compare) fills the symbol list with the 100 largest coins **by market cap that trade on Binance spot**. Ranking from CoinGecko `/coins/markets` (keyless, CORS-open, 250 fetched so 100 survive filtering); tradability from Binance `/ticker/24hr?type=MINI` — a pair must have traded in the last 24h, because Compare fetches Binance klines and anything else is just a skipped row. Duplicate tickers keep the higher-cap coin. **If CoinGecko fails, the list is ranked by Binance 24h volume and the confirm AND the persistent status line say so** (`cmpListNote`) — never pass volume off as market cap. **Ranked fresh on every click** (Oct 2026: a 24h cache made repeat clicks look hardcoded). `smb.top100` keeps the last good list only for when Binance can't be reached, and then the confirm and status line say how old it is. **Top 50 gainers** (`#cmpGainers`, Oct 2026) is Binance's own 24h ranking of USDT spot pairs (`/ticker/24hr?type=MINI`, `lastPrice/openPrice − 1`), also fetched fresh on every click. It adds a $1M 24h-volume floor (`GAIN_MIN_VOL`). Both buttons REPLACE the symbol list, and their confirm says so. **Both lists take NORMAL SPOT COINS only** (`isNormalCoin()`, user request Oct 2026). Binance spot also trades ~100 **tokenized stocks and ETFs** (AAPLB, NVDAB, SPYB, and leveraged/inverse ETF tokens like TQQQB, SQQQB, SOXLB, UVXYB). On live data the first gainers build took 12 of them into the top 50, and the volume fallback took 18. `spotUniverse()` reads two lists fresh on every click. The first is `exchangeInfo?permissions=SPOT&symbolStatus=TRADING&showPermissionSets=false`, Binance's currently-trading pairs (~55 KB gzipped). Every leveraged token (BTCUP, ETHDOWN, ETHBULL, …) is delisted (BREAK), so this list already drops them. The second is `permissions=TRD_GRP_261`, Binance's own trading group for the stock tokens (~4 KB; real coins sit in TRD_GRP_258). **Never filter these by name.** Real coins end in B (ARB, BNB, SHIB, CKB, DGB, TRB, BB, YB) and in UP (JUP, SYRUP), and stock tokens collide with crypto tickers (STXB is Seagate, not Stacks; BBB is BlackBerry, not BounceBit). If the group list can't be read, or stops looking like stock tokens (under 20 entries, or a name not ending in B), `STOCK_SNAPSHOT` (the 100 of Oct 2026) stands in. If the trading list can't be read, `isLevToken()` stands in: prefix + UP/DOWN/BULL/BEAR, where the prefix must itself be a listed coin. The status line (`coinsNote`) says when either fallback was used. A `confirm()` states the upper-bound cost first (`cmpEstimate`: pages × ~0.3 s ÷ 3 + `CMP_CPU_S` per coin). **Big lists change how `runCompare` runs**: over 12 symbols it keeps **3 coins in flight** (network overlaps compute; `fetchKlines` already backs off on 429/418, and 3 × ~5 req/s is far inside Binance's 6000-weight/min); up to 12 it is the old one-at-a-time path, unchanged. Over 25 symbols each coin's raw candles are **evicted from `cmpCache` once its rows are computed** — 100 coins × 20k bars is ~2M candle objects, enough to kill a phone tab; the rows are all the table needs, and changing Bars simply refetches. Measured with mocks on 4h: 100 coins, 400 rows, ~1,500 requests, never more than 3 in flight, 37 MB heap.
- **Portfolio tab** (`#pfView`, the default view): driven by an **orders ledger** — `localStorage` `smb.orders` = `[{id,sym,side:'buy'|'sell'|'open',qty,price,time,src}]`, **never committed, never uploaded**; don't hardcode anyone's positions into the repo. Holdings are DERIVED by `replay()`, oldest order first, **average-cost** method (buy blends the average, sell realises `(price−avg)×qty` and leaves the average alone, sold-to-zero resets it); fees are not modelled. A sell beyond the recorded buys realises only what is accounted for and is flagged (`short`), never given an invented cost. `side:'open'` is an **opening-balance snapshot** (the old typed-in holdings, migrated once from `smb.holdings`, which is left untouched): it SETS the position at its time and supersedes that coin's older orders, so imported history can never double-count a hand-typed balance; the screenshot importer offers to replace it, ticked by default. Two invariants: it **drops the forming candle** (`closeTime <= now`) so an alert never refers to a bar that can still change — the webapp deliberately keeps that bar (and marks any signal on it pending, see "Forming candle"), the bot must not — and it persists position state in `signals/state.json`, committed back by the workflow, so each flip fires once. **No open position is ever silent:** on a first sighting an entry on a candle closed after the previous persisted run (`state._meta.lastRun`, written only with a real change) is a normal BUY, an older one gets ONE `OPEN TRADE` note (levels + whether price is still within 1R); state's `noted` marks a position as announced, and positions recorded silently before notes existed get theirs on the next run. Closed history before you watched stays quiet. A state entry **older than 10 bars re-baselines silently** instead of alerting — otherwise removing a coin and re-adding it weeks later would announce a trade that opened, and maybe closed, while nobody was watching. Watchlist is data (`signals/watchlist.json`), not code. **Trading book (Oct 2026, user decision, ALGORITHM.md "The trading book"):** 5 equal slots (`signals/book.json`). Every signal, taken or not, is recorded in `signals/journal.json`, which the workflow commits with state (rebase-retry push). The user's Taken/Skipped answers are `signals/decisions.json`, written by the page; ids are `SYMBOL|tf|entryBarTime`. Nothing is sent inside the item loop: events are queued, then handled in phases. Order: sells/halves → rules notes → buys/opens, highest relative volume first → deferred halves → late entries for every open position without one → entries whose coin left the watchlist become `dropped`. A slot is held by Taken, or by alerted & unanswered. When all are held, a BUY is **journal-only, no alert**. An OPEN TRADE note needs a free slot AND price within 1R. SELL/TAKE HALF alert only for a held trade. **The repo never holds money amounts.** `--book/--journal/--decisions` (like `--state/--watch`) redirect the files for tests. Channels are additive and all optional (ntfy / Telegram / Discord), with a GitHub issue as the zero-setup fallback, which fires ONLY when no other channel is configured (otherwise every signal leaves a ticket to close). `--dry-run`, `--selftest` and `--fixture <path>` run it without a live market; `--fixture` is how the buy/sell transitions are tested. `--ping` (also a `workflow_dispatch` input) sends a test message through every configured channel and exits — the only way to verify a channel without waiting for a real trade; note `dry_run` sends nothing, so it is the wrong switch for that. **Secrets never go in `index.html`** — the page is public, so a bot token in client code is a token anyone owns; that is why alerts are sent from the workflow and not the browser. Setup and caveats in `signals/README.md`.
- **Alerts tab** (`#alView`, 4th tab) — edits `signals/watchlist.json`, the file the Action reads, from the phone. The page is static with no backend, so it cannot write to the repo alone; two routes exist and **the tokenless one must always work**: copy the JSON into GitHub's web editor, or save a fine-grained PAT in `localStorage` (`smb.ghToken`) and let Apply commit via the Contents API. A token is **never** written into the page or the repo — same rule as the bot secrets. The live list is re-fetched on EVERY visit to the tab via the **Contents API** (`raw.githubusercontent.com` is only the fallback and is flagged "may be up to 5 min old" — its CDN ignores cache-busters, which once left a just-committed list showing its old rows struck through), never assumed from local state. **A draft records the live list it started from (`smb.alBase`)**: if the repo moved on since, an unedited draft is dropped and an edited one raises a "draft is older than the bot's list" banner with Apply blocked until the user picks — Oct 2026 a stale phone draft committed through the web editor silently reverted 19 alerts. Drafts are deleted whenever they equal the live list, and the banner says `In sync` / `Not applied yet` with an add+remove count: a silent divergence would mean editing a list the bot has never seen. Removals render struck-through with an undo. Draft is `smb.alDraft`; owner/repo is derived from the Pages hostname, overridable via `smb.alRepo`. **One collapsible list per strategy** (Nov 2026, user request; `<details class="pfPanel alSec">` like the token panels): `AL_STRATS` gives each its name, a one-line description, its timeframes (liqbrk 15m–1d; Trend Follow / Turtle 4h, 1d; BTC Cycle 4h, 1d, default 4h since Nov 2026 at the user's request: it is daily on either, so the trades are identical) and, through `alBarsFor` / `alMinBars`, a default bar count past its warm-up and a floor (tsmom/donch need the 200-day MA first: 1,200 bars on 4h; the Cycle wants the 200-week MA, so its bars are counted in days: 3,000 days = 18,000 bars on 4h, floor 1,500 days). `alSection()` builds a section once and `alRender()` only refills it, so an open section and a half-typed coin survive edits. Headers show the count and what is not applied yet. The coins box takes several at once (`parseSyms`). `#alBody` is now the container of the sections. A strategy found on the list that `AL_STRATS` doesn't know gets a section without an add form.

## Strategies (dropdown → `strategy` param)
- `cycle` (default) — BTC halving playbook: staged accumulation at the 200-week MA (zone A + deep zone B, harmonic avg entry, stop 0.65×200w), Pi-Cycle / 40-week exits. **Rebuilt Oct 2026** (ALGORITHM.md top of the cycle section; study `research/cycle/RESULTS_cycle_2026-10.md`). The old code bought alts with the stop ABOVE the price and "sold" at that stop the next bar, above the whole candle. ADA 4h showed 60 trades, 49 impossible, $1k → $724M; the median over 209 coins was +7.5 million %. It lives in `runCycle()`, outside `runQuant`'s bar loop, and is a **daily system on every chart**: `dailyBars()` aggregates sub-daily candles to UTC days and the trades are mapped back onto the chart's bars, so 4h and 1d give identical trade dates. Don't move it back into the per-bar loop, where "5 days under the 40w" was counted in bars and one 4h close reset it. Rules: the stop is checked on the day's low BEFORE any tranche buy and fills at `min(open, stop)`; zone entries and tranche 2 need price ≥ 0.72×200w. **Alts follow BTC's cycle** through `params.btcD`, BTC DAILY candles with all history (the app's `btcDailyFor()`, ~4 pages, shared, 5-min refresh; `'self'` on BTCUSDT, where gating is a no-op). Without BTC data the gate is open and `advice.cycle.btc.missing` is set; the status line and advisor SAY so. Half is sold at the first close ≥ `cycleScale` (1.85) × the 200-day MA, never below the entry. Trades carry `fills` [{idx,w,px}], and `SMC.markMult` floats only the tranches filled by bar j, so drawdown isn't overstated before later tranches. `research/cycle/cycle_lab.js parity` checks the lab against the pre-rebuild engine (from git); `parity-final` checks it against today's engine (209/209 on 1d, 44/44 on 4h). Run both before touching the cycle. Honest result: the median alt still loses −7% at a −67% drawdown, against −85% / −98% for holding. It is a BTC playbook alts can borrow, not an alt picker.
- `tsmom`, `donch` — pure CTA trend / turtle breakout.
- `gold` — **long-term gold: the clean-trend system** (Nov 2026, user request; ALGORITHM.md "`gold`", `research/gold/`). Built for **XAUUSD** (spot gold, see below); picking it opens XAUUSD 1d with all its history (Bars ≥ 9000). PAXGUSDT works too. **Leaving Gold brings back the chart it replaced** (user request, Nov 2026): `preGold` keeps the symbol, TF and bars from before, and picking any other strategy restores them, but only while the chart is still the XAUUSD Gold opened. `run()` forgets `preGold` as soon as any other symbol is charted (typed, or opened from a journal row, holding card or Compare row, which set the strategy without a change event), so a coin you chose stays, and so does an XAUUSD you typed yourself.
  - Rules: every UTC calendar month gets the AVERAGE of its daily closes. In when the 9-month change is up AND Kaufman's efficiency ratio (net move ÷ sum of monthly moves) ≥ 0.30. Out when the change turns down OR the ratio falls under 0.15 (`goldMonths` 9, `goldER` 0.3).
  - It runs in `runGold()`, a monthly machine outside `runQuant`'s bar loop like `runCycle`, so 4h and 1d give the same trades. A month decides once a later month has started, so the running month never decides. A first month the data joins midway is dropped. Fills are at the month's last daily close.
  - **No stop.** `stop` carries the exit line (the average 9 months back), and the chart/Trades table call it that. The Advisor says when the next decision is and what it would do if the month ended now (`advice.gold.live`). The dashed gold steps on the chart are the monthly averages (`goldLines`).
  - How it was chosen: five candidates (seasonal, 52-week-high anchor, dollar tide, secular breakout, efficiency ratio) plus two retail baselines. Data: World Bank monthly averages, rules picked on 1975–2000, judged on 2001–26. A decision earns the averaged return two months on, because averages flatter trend rules.
  - Honest result: 1975–2026 +7.1%/yr at a −24% max drawdown, against +6.2% / −62% holding. It beats holding's MAR in 5 of 6 decades. But it only ties 12-month momentum out of sample, and it made LESS than holding on XAUUSD's daily candles 2003–26 (×5.56 vs ×11.42; daily drawdown −31% vs −45%, so the same return per unit of drawdown) and on PAXG 2020–26 (×1.40 vs ×2.00): it sidesteps long bears, it doesn't beat a bull.
  - `node research/gold/gold_lab.js parity` checks the engine against the lab on PAXG (1d, and 4h = 1d); `… xau` runs it on the XAUUSD file (real daily drawdown, and 1w / 1M = 1d). It is in neither Compare (`CMP_STRATS` is for the crypto lists) nor the bot.

  - Data: `data/xauusd_1d.csv`, Dukascopy BID daily candles from 2 Jan 2003, UTC days, with Sunday's evening session folded into Monday. It reaches YESTERDAY: a day's minute file only appears once the day ends.
  - It is built by `scripts/xauusd-data.py` (stdlib, LZMA `.bi5` files). It reads Dukascopy's own day candles for finished years (one file a year, ~30 s for the whole history with 4 workers), hour files for this year's finished months, and minute files for the running month (its hour file appears when the month ends). In January it re-reads last December too. `.github/workflows/xauusd.yml` re-reads the last two months six times a day and commits on change; `full` (dispatch input) rebuilds everything. A quote from Saturday, or Sunday before 22:00 UTC, adds nothing (`xauDayOf()`); Sunday's evening session is Monday's candle. The status line always names the source and how far the file reaches.

## Conventions
- Run locally: `python -m http.server 8910` (or preview config `bos-backtester` in `.claude/launch.json`).
- Benchmarks: Node scripts that `require('./detector.js')`, fetch Binance daily klines, compute all-in equity with 0.1%/side fees, marked-to-market daily drawdown. Always compare vs buy & hold and report WR/DD/CAGR per symbol.
- The user judges strategies by: win rate ≥30%, drawdown, consistency across ≥5 symbols. Don't overfit weak assets (XRP/LINK fail deliberately).
- Equity panel is exposure-based (`Invest %` of equity per hold, 100% = all-in), NOT risk-per-trade sizing.
- If a rule changes, change `ALGORITHM.md` first.
- **Validate on ALL available history, not the cached window.** ZEC 15m reaches back to 2019-03-21 (~258k bars). A 2-year bull slice made `liqbrk` look like a 13.5%-drawdown system when it is really a 61%-drawdown one.
- **There is NO Binance bar-count ceiling.** `fetchKlines` pages backwards via `endTime`, so depth is limited only by the pair's listing date — BTCUSDT 4h reaches 2017-08-17 (19,959 bars, **9.1 years, two halving cycles**). The old `10000` was a self-imposed `max=` on the Bars inputs; a code comment mistook it for an API limit and that error propagated into ALGORITHM.md. Both inputs now allow 60,000. **This mistake cost real accuracy:** the 4.6-year 4h window reported BTC's `liqbrk` drawdown as −14% when over two cycles it is **−39%** (BNB −19%→−38%, DOGE −39%→−74%). Always quote the deep-window drawdown. Encouragingly, window *stability* held or improved on 7 of 10 symbols over two cycles — the short window flattered risk, not the edge.
- **A robust edge is not the same as a worthwhile one — always check `vs B&H`.** Over 9.1 years on 4h, `liqbrk` on BTC returned +837% while holding BTC returned ~+1,800%: trading halved the outcome. Same on LINK/BNB/SOL. The Sep 2026 "BTC is the best symbol" conclusion tested robustness of the edge and never asked whether it beat the asset. It wins outright on assets that chopped or round-tripped (XRP +1,323% vs B&H, AVAX +595%, DOT +491%). Counterweight: it is only in the market **7–13% of bars** at −25% to −44% drawdown vs buy & hold's ~−77%, so BTC is "half the money for an eighth of the exposure" — judge by whether the idle capital has somewhere else to be.
- **Never pick a symbol, or judge a strategy, from one Bars setting.** Measured Sep 2026 (ALGORITHM.md): at 650 bars on 1h `liqbrk` averages ~1 trade per window (DOGE: zero) and its win rate ranges 0–100% purely on where the window ends; even at 4350 bars about half of all window placements disagree on whether a symbol is profitable. What actually separates winners from losers is **regime** (BTC above/below its own 200-day MA: pooled PF 1.71 vs 1.00 on 1h), validated out of sample 4/4. BTC is the only symbol whose edge survives dropping its three best trades on both 1h and 2h.
- `pytester/` — Python desktop tester (Tkinter, no extra deps, `python -m pytester.app`). No bar cap, parquet cache, sweeps, folds, equity curves. **`detector.js` stays the source of truth**; `python pytester/verify.py` must print `ALL STRATEGIES MATCH detector.js` before trusting a number from it.
- **Scanning all Binance coins for the next top gainers is dead too (Oct 2026, ALGORITHM.md REJECTED):** volume/momentum signals find big movers but not their direction; top-3 rotation and an hourly day-trade of volume bursts (108 TP/SL/time settings, walk-forward, delisted coins included) lost in every setting, even with flattering fills.
- **SMC profit-taking for `liqbrk` is rejected (Oct 2026, ALGORITHM.md REJECTED, `research/smcexit/`):** this means selling at resting BSL or a major bearish FVG instead of ½ at +3R. Inside a liqbrk trade a BSL sweep continues MORE often than a plain level, and the nearest BSL sits ~0.6R above a breakout entry, so a BSL target is just "sell early"; random levels at the same distances beat it. FVG exits carry a little information (6/6 vs their controls) but fail the both-halves test on the bot's 61 coins. The shipped exit stays. Any future exit idea needs a distance-matched control, and the oos coins (where liqbrk loses, PF 0.89) flatter any rule that exits sooner. What the BSL rows really measured is selling half EARLY: plain ½ at +0.5R / +1R lifts WR 37% → 54% / 43% and cuts median DD −53% → −44% / −45% at statistically unchanged money (RESULTS §5). Measured, not shipped.
- **15m is not better than 4h for any strategy (Oct 2026, ALGORITHM.md RESEARCH, `research/tfcmp/`):** on the 103-coin list over the last 4 years, a 15m row ends with ~23% less money than the 4h row for liqbrk (win rate 41% → 28%, 22 vs 6 trades a coin a year, ~3× the slippage cost). BTC Cycle is identical (daily on every chart), and Turtle / Trend Follow are a coin flip. The study reads `pathStats` out of index.html, and a browser check matched 32/32 Compare rows.
- **liqbrk's 50-day BTC gate is logical on 15m too (Nov 2026, ALGORITHM.md RESEARCH, `research/liqbrk15m/`):** with no gate, 15m breaks even (16,265 trades, PF 1.00). The trades the gate blocks average −0.42% against +0.24% kept, and the gain sits in weak-BTC markets (2021–22 basket −6% → +22%). Faster BTC reads alone let the bears through. Also requiring BTC above its own 149-bar MA helped a little (+0.22% → +0.29% a trade): measured, NOT shipped. 15m stays weak even so (win rate 29%, PF without the top 2% of trades 0.79).
- **The 15m strategy that makes money is the 4h breakout read on 15m candles (Nov 2026, ALGORITHM.md RESEARCH, `research/s15/`; NOT shipped):** the shipped 15m Breakout fails because its volume test, stop and trail count 15m CANDLES. Keeping the 4h rules and buying at the first 15m close where the forming 4h candle qualifies (`H4e`) gives a median coin of +23.8% over all history (+18% at 0.10% slippage, 65/103 coins, PF 1.52), about the 4h chart's +24.7%, with entries up to 3¾h earlier and a deeper drawdown. Order flow (taker-buy share), sweep-and-reclaim dip buys and retest entries were tested and rejected.
- **Day trading is dead on this pair (tested July 2026).** Three day-trade variants (intraday breakout, momentum pullback, squeeze expansion) all lost to the swing system (best PF 1.28 vs 2.66). At a ~0.6% average move the 0.2% round-trip fee eats 34% of it, and a 12h hold cap destroys the fat tail that carries the edge (top-5 trades = 44% of gross profit). Don't retry without maker rebates or a proven higher-frequency edge.
- **ZEC 15m is a momentum market, not a mean-reversion one.** A forward-return scan (ALGORITHM.md `liqbrk`) shows top-of-range / above-VWAP / high-RSI states return 2–3× baseline monotonically, while buying "discount" returns *less than random*. Don't build dip-buying or sweep-fade intraday strategies here — three were built and all lost in every fold.
- Warm-up in `runQuant` is **per strategy** (`warm`). Don't reintroduce a global `maLen` guard: on 15m the 200-day MA is 19,200 bars, which silently zeroes out any intraday strategy.
- Advisor panel leads with plain language (% of capital deployed, entry price/date, live P/L); technical readouts live in the collapsed "Technical detail" section below.
